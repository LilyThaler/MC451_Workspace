## Reflection
I am familiar with the components of a story due to my creative writing background, but I've never considered how similar that structure is to that of a research paper. I feel like this mindset will indeed help me write my paper because I find it much easier to tell a story than I do trying to just explain data and hoping it makes sense. Since I'm in the accelerated master's program, I have had to write one small original research paper for Media Ethics, where I interviewed media professionals about their thoughts on AI in movies. I only had a handful of people in my data set because it was a qualitative setting, so I'm a bit intimidated to put together thousands of pieces of data. This chapter also got me thinking about what anomalies I have noticed in media that I might want to look into for this project. I'm a big fan of YouTube so I think I could find a topic on there pretty easily, and I'm also really fascinated by what I feel like is a trend of more independent films making it to movie theaters because of an online fanbase, like Iron Lung and the Backrooms. It also makes me feel more reassured to keep in mind that research is a constantly ongoing thing, and that my contribution will still be important regardless of if my hypothesis is proven to be right or not, and with my background in storytelling, I feel confident that I will be able to deal with the potential null hypothesis.
